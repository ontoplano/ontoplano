# The ontoplano name and mark

The code in this repository is one thing. The name **ontoplano**, the logo, and
the look of ontoplano.com are another, and they are not covered by whatever
licence the code carries.

## What you may do

Fork it. Change it. Run it for yourself, for your family, for your company.
Host it for other people. Charge for it. None of that needs anyone's
permission, and the project is built on the assumption that people will —
self-hosting is not a grudging concession here, it is the point.

You may say plainly and factually what your thing is built from: "based on
ontoplano", "a fork of ontoplano", "powered by ontoplano". That is accurate and
it is welcome.

## What you may not do

Call it ontoplano. Use the name, the mark, or a name close enough to be
mistaken for it, as the name of your service, your product, your company or
your app listing. Use the logo as your logo. Present your instance in a way
that would leave a reasonable person thinking they had arrived at this project,
or that its author stands behind what you are running.

The short version: **take the work, not the identity.** A fork under its own
name is a fork. A fork under this one is a claim about who wrote it and who is
answerable for it, and that claim would not be true.

## Why it is written down

Not to stop anyone using this. It is here because a name is the one part of a
project that cannot be forked without taking something from the person who
built it: if two different things are called ontoplano, then the word tells
nobody anything, and the one people trusted first is the one that pays for it.
An open licence gives away the work. It does not give away the answer to
"whose is this?"

If you want to use the name for something and are not sure whether it falls on
the right side of this, ask. The answer is often yes.
