# Ethos

Ontoplano is one app for a whole life: the week, the tasks, the notes, the
home, the money. These are the rules it keeps while doing that. When a
feature and one of these disagree, the feature changes.

## Your life is yours

Everything you write can be exported, with its pictures, and imported again
somewhere else. Everything can be deleted, and deleting means gone. You can
run your own instance on your own machine, or keep the whole app on your
phone with no server at all. The code is AGPL-3.0, so that stays true of
every copy.

## One place, one way of doing things

A to-do, a note, a thing in the cupboard and a bill are different objects
that live in the same app, so they behave the same way. A row looks like
every other row. `j` and `k` walk every list. Anything you can create, you can
edit, rename, reorder and delete. A thing put aside is archived, whatever room
it is in. Learning one room means knowing all of them.

A subject you are working through — a renovation, a trip, a book — is a
notebook, and it holds whatever that subject collects: its tasks, its notes,
its shopping, its bills, its goals. The point of one app is that these stop
living in five.

## Writing it down is faster than forgetting it

Capture is one gesture: hold the button in the bar, flick towards what it is,
type. Closing the box halfway keeps what you wrote. The fastest path in the
app is the one you take ten times a day.

## Nothing moves under your finger

Pressing something never shifts what you were about to press next. Space for
anything that can appear is kept before it appears; a count that changes
holds its width; a press is answered on screen before the server answers it.
An app you can trust is one where the button is where it was a moment ago.

## Opinions are opt-in

Some features are one person's way of living: three daily wins, a quote on
the dashboard. Those start off, and turning them on is a choice in your
preferences. A new account opens on something plain that it can grow into.

## An assistant is welcome, never required

Ontoplano works completely without AI. When you do connect one, it gets a key
with exactly the permissions you tick, and that key can be tied to a single
notebook. Deleting is a separate grant you have to give on purpose. Every
change an assistant makes is answered with what was there before, so it can
be undone.

## Colour is yours

The bright colours in the app belong to your categories and tags. The app's
own chrome stays quiet so it never competes with them. Colour is never the
only cue: anything coloured also carries its word, and the app never relies
on telling red from green.
